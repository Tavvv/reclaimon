A screen time app that works with Apple Health

You already tracked the workout on your watch. You already did the breathing session in another app. So why is your focus app showing zero? Here's how ReclaimOn counts the effort you actually made — wherever you made it.

Quick answer

Most screen time and focus apps only count minutes inside their own timer. ReclaimOn also reads workouts and mindful minutes from Apple Health and counts them toward your weekly goals automatically. Effort recorded by your Apple Watch or another app lands on the same scoreboard — nothing to start, nothing to log twice.

The problem with app-only counting

Every focus app has a scoreboard, and almost every scoreboard has the same blind spot: it only sees what happens inside the app itself. Meditate with the timer your watch already has? Zero. Go for a run tracked the way you always track runs? Zero. Do the exact thing your goal asked for, in the tool you already trust for it — and your "progress" app shrugs.

That blind spot does real damage. Goals start to feel like bookkeeping: do the thing, then remember to also tell the app you did the thing. Miss the second step and the streak says you failed on a day you actually showed up. Plenty of good habits die of paperwork.

Effort you already made counts

ReclaimOn connects to Apple Health, the place your iPhone and Apple Watch already keep this record. Two kinds of effort count automatically:

Workouts. If your goal is to move — run, lift, swim, walk — a workout recorded on your Apple Watch or in any app that writes to Apple Health counts toward that goal. Start the workout the way you always start it. That's it.

Mindful minutes. Meditation and breathing sessions logged in Apple Health — from the Mindfulness app on your watch or from third-party apps — count toward a mindfulness goal. Sit down, breathe the way you usually breathe, and the minutes are already on the board.

One scoreboard, however you got there

Inside ReclaimOn, those automatic minutes sit alongside the ones you earn in anchored sessions — the reading hour at 9pm, the morning meditation at 6am. The weekly number on your home screen is the honest total: time from your anchors, time from Reclaim More sessions you started on the fly, and time your watch recorded while you were busy actually doing the thing.

It matters because the number means something. When the week says 240 minutes, that's 240 minutes of your life pointed where you meant it to go — not 240 minutes of remembering to press start in one particular app.

Your health data stays on your iPhone

Apple Health data is read locally, on your device, and it never leaves it. ReclaimOn has no accounts, no sign-in, and no server that your workouts or mindful minutes are ever sent to — the same rule the rest of the app lives by. You grant access once in Settings, and you can revoke it the same way at any time.

Every minute counts. Even the ones you already did.

FAQ

Which screen time apps work with Apple Health?

Very few — most only count time inside their own sessions. ReclaimOn reads workouts and mindful minutes from Apple Health and counts them toward your weekly goals automatically.

Can Apple Watch workouts count toward my goals?

Yes. A workout recorded on your Apple Watch lands in Apple Health and ReclaimOn counts it toward the matching goal — no timer to start, nothing to log twice.

What are mindful minutes?

Time recorded in Apple Health by meditation and breathing apps, including Apple's Mindfulness app on Apple Watch. ReclaimOn counts them toward a mindfulness goal alongside your anchored sessions.

Does my health data leave my phone?

No. It's read locally and stays on your device. ReclaimOn has no accounts and no server that receives your health data.

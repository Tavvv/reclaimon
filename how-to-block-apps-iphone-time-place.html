How to block apps on iPhone at a specific time & place

You don't want your phone blocked all day — you want Instagram locked at 9pm when you said you'd read, or TikTok off limits at your desk. Here are the three ways to do it, from Apple's built-in tools to fully automatic.

Quick answer

iPhone gives you two built-in options: Screen Time (App Limits and Downtime) blocks apps on a daily schedule, and a scheduled Focus mode can switch on at a time or location. Neither combines time and place with real blocking. For that, an app like ReclaimOn anchors blocking to a window and a place — it switches on when you arrive and off when you leave, automatically.

Option 1: Block apps at a set time with Screen Time

Apple's Screen Time has two tools that work on a clock:

Downtime blocks everything except apps you mark "Always Allowed" during a daily window:

  1. Open Settings → Screen Time and turn it on if you haven't
  2. Tap Downtime and set your window — say 9pm to 7am
  3. Under Always Allowed, keep only what you truly need (Phone, Messages)

App Limits cap total minutes per day on apps you choose: Settings → Screen Time → App Limits → Add Limit, pick the apps or categories, set the allowance.

Both are free and already on your phone. The catches: App Limits count a daily total rather than protecting a specific hour, everything resets at midnight, and any limit can be extended with one tap and a passcode — usually your own passcode, typed by you, at your weakest moment.

Option 2: Schedule a Focus mode by time or location

Focus modes are the only built-in feature that can trigger on place as well as time:

  1. Open Settings → Focus and tap + to create one (or edit an existing mode)
  2. Choose which people and apps may notify you — everything else stays quiet
  3. Under Set a Schedule, add a Time, a Location (like your office or gym), or an app that triggers it

Focus modes are excellent at silencing noise and can even hide Home Screen pages. But they don't hard-block: the apps are still there, one tap away, with no friction between impulse and open. And a location schedule still can't say "only between 6 and 8am." Place or time — not both.

Where the built-in tools stop

If your goal is "no social apps at my kitchen table after 9pm," you need three things at once: the right apps, the right window, and the right place. Screen Time covers the first two loosely; Focus covers the last two loosely. Nothing built in ties all three together, and everything built in can be overridden in the moment by the person it was meant to restrain — you.

That's not a settings problem. It's a design gap: the tools assume you'll make the right call every night, at the exact hour your willpower is lowest.

FAQ

Can you block apps on iPhone at a certain time?

Yes. Downtime blocks all but your allowed apps on a daily schedule, and App Limits cap daily minutes per app. For blocking tied to a specific window and place, a ReclaimOn Habit Anchor does both at once, automatically.

Can you block apps by location on iPhone?

Not with Screen Time — it schedules by time only. A Focus mode can activate at a location but only silences notifications. ReclaimOn is built around place: arrive and the apps shield, leave and they release.

Do iPhone App Limits reset every day?

Yes, at midnight. They measure a daily total rather than protecting a set hour, and they can be extended with a tap — which is why many people add a stricter blocker for the windows that matter.

What's the easiest way to block apps automatically?

Decide once, then let the environment enforce it: pick a goal, choose the apps, set a time and a place. Blocking switches itself on and off every day without you touching a setting again.
